\subsection{A conservative method-selection rule}
\label{sec:decision-rule}

The method results do not support a single architecture-invariant remedy. They
do support a conservative gate before deployment. First, measure strict
set-leaving at the target horizon. All four models here exceed 0.53, so static
top-1\% protection needs validation rather than assumption. Second, test
budgeted EMA against a matched static top-10 control. Nemotron passes this
gate: M11b top-10 recovers 0.815 with CI [0.254, 0.923] and beats static
top-10 by 0.220 median. The same gate is ambiguous on DeepSeek and Falcon:
top-10 recovery is 0.335 with CI [-0.409, 0.494] on DeepSeek and 0.044 with
CI [-0.144, 0.203] on Falcon. Third, when a rotation baseline is available,
compare it directly rather than stacking it with M11b. Granite favors
ParoQuant over M11b, and ParoQuant+M11b is sub-additive.

This is a practical selection rule rather than a universal predictor. Use
rotation where it is validated, as on Granite; use budgeted EMA where it
clears a model-local matched-budget gate, as on Nemotron; and avoid claiming a
channel-set positive method when high drift is present but the matched-budget
gate is ambiguous, as on DeepSeek and Falcon. The rule turns the negative
composition result into deployment guidance: remedies must be selected by
measured model behavior, not stacked by default.
