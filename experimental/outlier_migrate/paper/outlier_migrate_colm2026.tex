\title{Channel-Set Drift in Long-Reasoning W4A16 LLMs: Mechanisms and a Budget-Tuned Remedy}
\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Static per-channel protection is attractive for W4A16 reasoning inference, but it assumes that high-magnitude activation channels remain useful protection targets throughout decode. We measure decode-time channel-set drift across four reasoning LLMs and find strict top-1\% set-leaving of 53--67\% by horizons up to 20K tokens. The effect appears at the measured block-output surface in hybrid Mamba-2, parallel-hybrid, and pure-Transformer models, extending DecDEC's short-horizon Transformer observation and contradicting the channel-persistence assumption behind Quamba2-style static ordering in our long-decode setting. We then test seven channel-set protection families with matched-cost and random controls. Hard position or scale switches are actively harmful, top-1\% smoothing and cross-tensor coupling have signal but insufficient recovery, and reactive DecDEC-style selection does not recover the W4A16 gap. Budget-tuned EMA protection is the strongest channel-set remedy: on Nemotron-3, top-10 protection recovers 0.82 of the BF16-vs-static gap and beats static top-10 by 0.22; on Granite-Small, top-5 recovers 0.45 with a wide CI. A ParoQuant rotation baseline recovers 0.75 on Granite-Small, scoping our negative results to channel-set protection rather than W4A16 PTQ generally. KL trajectories grow sublinearly with AR decays 0.44--0.51, weakening compound-error explanations in the measured regime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long-reasoning models can spend thousands of decode steps in a single answer, so calibration-time quantization decisions must survive within-trace distribution shift. Static protected-channel maps are especially brittle if the channels that dominate early activation magnitude do not remain dominant later. This paper studies that assumption for W4A16 post-training quantization (PTQ): 4-bit weights, 16-bit activations, and a small protected activation-channel set kept at higher precision.

The assumption matters for recent state-space quantization. Quamba2 argues that Mamba-family activations exhibit channel-order preservation and activation persistence, properties that support static channel ordering and clustering \citep{chiang2025quamba2}. We test the corresponding protected-set assumption at long reasoning horizons. Across Granite-4.0-H-Small, Nemotron-3-Nano, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, and Falcon-H1, 53--67\% of the top-1\% channels at the calibration position strictly leave the later top-1\% set. This is not merely within-set rank shuffling; it is membership change in the set a static policy would protect.

The systems motivation is narrow but practical. Reasoning workloads are expensive, and inference-cost forecasts expect large pressure to reduce per-token cost even as reasoning demand grows \citep{bort2025reasoningcosts,gartner2026inferencecost}. Recent W4A16 systems for larger standard Transformers can recover substantial quality, including ParoQuant's rotation-based reasoning results \citep{liang2025paroquant}. Our focus is different: small reasoning models, hybrid Mamba-2 or parallel-hybrid architectures, pure PTQ rather than distillation, and channel-set protection under long decode.

Our contributions are fourfold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Empirical drift.} We provide a four-model cross-architecture measurement of decode-time channel-set drift up to 20K tokens, with strict set-leaving of 53--67\% across hybrid Mamba-2, parallel-hybrid, and pure-Transformer models.
\item \textbf{Prior-art differentiation.} We extend DecDEC's short-horizon Transformer evidence to the long-reasoning regime and show that Quamba2's channel-persistence assumption fails at the measured block-output surface for the hybrid models tested here.
\item \textbf{Mechanism tests.} We evaluate seven channel-set protection families with random and matched-cost controls, yielding a three-mechanism account: boundary discontinuities are harmful, smooth top-1\% tracking is insufficient, and protected budget is load-bearing; KL accumulation does not support runaway compound error in the measured Granite packet.
\item \textbf{Partial remedy and scope.} Budget-tuned EMA protection succeeds on Nemotron at top-10 and shows partial Granite signal, while ParoQuant recovers 0.75 on Granite via rotation. Thus the negative result is scoped to channel-set protection, not all W4A16 PTQ.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Mamba and state-space quantization.} Vision-Mamba quantization already reports dynamic activation outliers: QMamba introduces temporal grouping for hidden states \citep{li2025qmamba}, and OuroMamba uses dynamic outlier detection during inference \citep{ramachandran2025ouromamba}. Language-Mamba work often assumes more stable structure. Mamba-PTQ identifies recurrent outlier channels \citep{pierro2024mambaptq}; Quamba and Quamba2 use static or calibration-time structure for Mamba-family PTQ \citep{chiang2024quamba,chiang2025quamba2}; MambaQuant uses variance-aligned rotations \citep{xu2025mambaquant}. Our measurement asks whether the static protected-set assumption holds in long reasoning traces.\footnote{We use \emph{decode-time channel-set drift} for the measured phenomenon. SmoothQuant's ``migration'' is activation-to-weight scale transfer \citep{xiao2023smoothquant}; MoBiQuant uses related language for token-set shifts across bit-widths \citep{wang2026mobiquant}.}

\paragraph{LLM quantization and rotations.} SmoothQuant, AWQ, QuaRot, KVQuant, and BlockDialect protect or transform activation structure using calibration-time statistics, rotations, or adaptive formats \citep{xiao2023smoothquant,lin2023awq,ashkboos2024quarot,hooper2024kvquant,jang2025blockdialect}. These systems are not contradicted by our results. The implication is narrower: if the protected channel set changes during long decode, static channel maps need validation on the specific architecture and horizon. ParoQuant is particularly important because it reports strong W4A16 recovery for reasoning models through pairwise rotations \citep{liang2025paroquant}; our ParoQuant baseline contextualizes, rather than replaces, the channel-set experiments.

\paragraph{Dynamic and long-context policies.} DecDEC dynamically identifies salient activation channels at each decode step and reports low recall for static outlier-channel identification in short Transformer decode \citep{park2025decdec}. Our novelty is horizon, model class, architecture, and reasoning benchmark surface. KV-cache methods such as KIVI, PM-KVQ, AttentionPredictor, ChanMix, and MixKVQ adapt or compress cached key/value tensors \citep{liu2024kivi,liu2025pmkvq,yang2025attentionpredictor,liao2026chanmix,zhang2025mixkvq}; our interventions target linear-layer activation channels.\footnote{``Hot channels'' in HCP/CHON refers to persistently extreme channels during NVFP4 pretraining, which differs from our W4A16 inference-time setting. We refer to arXiv:2603.19296 as \emph{test-time TTQ} to avoid conflict with classical Trained Ternary Quantization.}

\paragraph{Error accumulation.} SLQ finds that near-lossless quantization errors need not compound across token positions \citep{helcig2026slq}. QEP, LAQuant, and Activation Sensitivity emphasize layer-wise propagation, lookahead losses, calibration mismatch, and task-conditional sensitivity as PTQ risks \citep{arai2025qep,choi2026laquant,xu2026activationsensitivity}. We therefore test, rather than assume, compound error in our aggressive W4A16 endpoint setting.

\section{Method}

For each trace, layer, and measured decode position, we record the vector of per-channel activation magnitudes. Let $S_{\ell,p}(t)$ be the top-1\% channel set for layer $\ell$ and prompt $p$ at position $t$. The strict set-leaving rate at position $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  1 - \frac{|S_{\ell,p}(100) \cap S_{\ell,p}(t)|}{|S_{\ell,p}(100)|},
\end{equation}
averaged over prompts and layers. Within-set shuffling is reported separately for channels that remain in the top-1\% set but move substantially in rank.

We score interventions using recovery of the BF16-vs-static W4A16 gap:
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{L_{\text{static}} - L_{\text{method}}}{L_{\text{static}} - L_{\text{BF16}}}.
\end{equation}
Recovery may be negative when a method is worse than static protection and may exceed one when a method beats BF16 on the scored slice. We therefore report unclipped medians and bootstrap CIs. Traces with no recoverable static gap are tracked explicitly and excluded from ratio computation for method CIs; their frequency is part of the trace-heterogeneity result.

We test seven channel-set method classes: static migration-aware unions, hard position-conditioned protected sets, hard position-binned scale tables, EMA-smoothed top-magnitude protection (M11), cross-tensor activation+key coupling (M18), DecDEC-style reactive top-1\% selection, budget-tuned EMA protection (M11b), and stable-core protection (M26). Each method is compared to static baselines plus random or matched-budget controls when applicable. ParoQuant is evaluated as an algorithmic rotation baseline, not as a channel-set method.

\section{Experiments}

\paragraph{Models and traces.} The measurement suite covers Granite-4.0-H-Small, Nemotron-3-Nano, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, and Falcon-H1. Intervention experiments use 12 deterministic long-reasoning traces unless otherwise noted; the original drift measurement uses 24 Granite-Small traces. Decode positions are measured on the grid $\{100,500,1000,5000,10000,20000\}$ when the model packet supports 20K tokens. Dense KL analysis uses a Granite-Small packet sampled every 100 positions to 10K.

\paragraph{Controls.} Static top-1\% protection is the baseline. Position- and scale-switching methods use random-bin controls. EMA and reactive methods use random-walk or random-reactive controls. Budget experiments include matched static top-10 controls. This control design is load-bearing: two methods that look plausible in isolation are worse than their random controls.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Channel sets drift at long reasoning horizons}

Figure~\ref{fig:set-leaving} shows the headline measurement. All four models exhibit increasing strict set-leaving across decode position. By the final measured position, strict set-leaving is 0.566 for Granite-Small, 0.534 for Nemotron-3, 0.671 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill, and 0.674 for Falcon-H1. The pure-Transformer reference drifts at least as much as the hybrid models, so the phenomenon is not confined to Mamba layers.

\subsection{Layer type does not explain the drift away}

The Quamba2 contradiction is strongest if drift appears in SSM/Mamba block outputs, not only in attention or MLP outputs. Figure~\ref{fig:components} shows that Granite and Nemotron block-output drift is comparable across layer types. Granite attention and SSM/Mamba outputs have strict set-leaving of 0.557 and 0.567. Nemotron attention, MoE, and SSM/Mamba outputs have 0.563, 0.527, and 0.533. This does not isolate internal SSM state tensors, but it does show that the measured block-output surface is not channel-persistent.

\subsection{Channel-set interventions mostly fail at top-1\% budget}

Figure~\ref{fig:method-recovery} and Table~\ref{tab:methods} summarize the intervention outcomes. Hard position-conditioned protection is outperformed by its random-bin control by 0.668 median recovery, and hard scale bins are outperformed by random scale-bin assignment by 0.761. EMA smoothing is different: M11 beats its random-walk control by 1.25 median, but its absolute recovery is only 0.048. M18 activation+key coupling has the strongest signal-vs-random margin among failed top-1\% methods, beating random coupling by 4.32 median, but its absolute recovery is negative. DecDEC-style reactive selection is less bad than a random reactive control but still does not recover quality. M26 stable-core protection shows signal over random matched-core channels but remains below the positive-method threshold.

\subsection{Budget is load-bearing, but not model-invariant}

M11b changes the conclusion from uniformly negative to partially positive. On Granite-Small, top-5 EMA protection recovers 0.449 of the static gap and beats static top-10 by 0.501 median, but the CI is wide. On Nemotron, top-5 is positive with a strictly positive CI, yet it does not beat static top-10. The top-10 arm is the strongest channel-set result: median recovery 0.815, CI [0.254, 0.923], and a 0.220 median margin over static top-10. Thus budget is a real mechanism, but the best budget and the value over static protection are model-dependent.

ParoQuant constrains the claim. Its Granite-Small rotation baseline recovers 0.754, exceeding M11b top-5 on the same model. The paper therefore does not argue that W4A16 PTQ is intrinsically broken; it argues that channel-set protection is fragile under long reasoning drift, while rotation-based conditioning can address part of the same endpoint by a different mechanism.

\subsection{KL does not support runaway compounding}

Figure~\ref{fig:kl} tests whether failed channel-set methods primarily fail because errors compound over decode. In the dense Granite-Small packet, static top-1\%, DecDEC proxy, and M11 all select a sublinear square-root fit over linear or superlinear alternatives. Mean KL is 0.150, 0.133, and 0.131 respectively, and fitted AR decays are 0.51, 0.45, and 0.44. This weakens compound error as the primary explanation in this measured regime and shifts attention back to stale or misbudgeted protection.

\section{Discussion}

The results support a three-mechanism account. First, boundary discontinuities are actively harmful: both hard protected-set and hard scale-bin switching lose to random controls. Second, smoothing removes the worst boundary harm but does not recover quality at top-1\% budget; M11 and M18 contain signal, but not enough protected capacity. Third, budget is binding, but budget alone is not a universal recipe: Nemotron top-10 succeeds, Granite top-5 is promising but high-variance, and static top-10 is already strong on Nemotron.

Trace heterogeneity remains important. In the Granite channel-set experiments, roughly one third of the 12-trace slice has no recoverable static gap under the ratio metric; in the larger static-union experiment the no-gap fraction is 0.375. This limits median-based claims and explains the wide CIs on several methods. The paper therefore emphasizes controlled mechanism comparisons and cross-model directionality rather than a single universal method headline.

The natural next step is composition rather than another threshold-only channel set. ParoQuant shows that rotations can recover much of the Granite W4A16 gap; M11b shows that protected budget matters. A stronger ICLR follow-up would test rotation plus budget-aware protection, larger reasoning scales, and training-time or calibration-time interventions that make long-decode channel statistics less brittle.

\section{Limitations}

The scope is W4A16 PTQ on small reasoning models and measured block-output activation channels. We do not claim results for FP8, MXFP4/NVFP4, W4A4, or quantization-aware training. The hybrid-model Quamba2 contradiction is at the accessible block-output surface, not at every internal SSM state tensor. Only Granite-Small has dense FFT and KL diagnostics; four-model dense spectral and per-position KL replication remain future work. The M11b positive result is cross-model but not model-invariant: Nemotron top-10 succeeds, while Granite's strongest signal is top-5 with wide uncertainty.

\section{Conclusion}

Long-reasoning W4A16 channel protection fails when static protected sets drift, and our experiments show that this drift is substantial across four measured models. Budget-tuned smoothing partially remedies the failure, especially on Nemotron, but rotations and broader calibration changes remain the stronger path toward a deployable method.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work analyzes benchmark prompts and model activations. It does not use private user data or make deployment-safety claims. The main ethical risk is overclaiming a scoped mechanism study as a universal quantization recipe; the paper therefore separates measurement, mechanism, and partial-remedy claims.
